Turnaround time

Halfway up a mountain, the real question is when to turn back. This takes your pace so far, the climb ahead and the daylight left, and gives a time, along with how sure it is. Planning around uncertainty is the same design problem as showing what an AI is unsure of.

A planning aid, not a promise. Weather, the ground underfoot and how you feel matter more than any number. If in doubt, turn back earlier.

How it works

  • Your pace, not an averageEffort is counted with Naismith's rule: an hour per 5 km, plus 10 minutes per 100 m up, plus 10 minutes per 300 m down. How long your effort so far took becomes your pace for the rest.
  • 2 000 versions of the afternoonNobody keeps a steady pace. The rest of the day is played out 2 000 times with a pace that wanders, tiredness that builds and descents that vary more than climbs. The more you've walked, the less your pace wanders.
  • A time, with its doubtThe turnaround is the latest moment when turning back still gets you down before sunset, minus your margin, in 9 runs out of 10. You can change both the margin and how sure it has to be.
  • Said plainlyOne time leads, in large type, because that's the decision. Ranges and chances come after, in words before numbers, the way you'd want an AI to admit what it doesn't know.
