Research to prototype: a fitness study

One loop, start to finish: search real apps on Mobbin, read every screen, pull the patterns into a moodboard, turn them into a visual language and six guidelines, then build a prototype that follows them. The prototype looks nothing like my site on purpose. Its look comes from the research. Made with Claude Code and the Mobbin MCP.

00

Process

I set the brief and made the calls. Claude Code did the searching, reading and building, through the Mobbin MCP and a browser it could screenshot. Each step fed the next one.

  1. 1. BriefA general fitness and health app, not a niche one. Find out what these apps say to you on an ordinary day.
  2. 2. SearchFour plain-language searches on Mobbin, one per moment of the day, eight iOS screens each.
  3. 3. ReadClaude looked at all 32 screens and named what kept repeating. Eight patterns survived.
  4. 4. MoodboardOne tile per pattern, each linked to the screens it came from.
  5. 5. Language and rulesThe look (ground, cards, accent, type) taken from what the apps share. Six guidelines from where they differ.
  6. 6. PrototypeThree screens, built to the language and the rules, checked in a browser, then cut back.

01

Research

32 screens from 22 apps, including Apple Fitness, Garmin Connect, Fitbit, Oura, WHOOP, Bevel, Visible, Gentler Streak, Peloton, Ladder, Runna, Hevy and Strava. These were the searches, word for word:

  1. daily activity summary with progress rings or bars for steps, workouts and sleep
  2. workout in progress screen with large timer, current exercise and next exercise
  3. recovery or readiness score screen with explanation of sleep and heart rate variability
  4. workout complete summary screen with duration, effort and a short reflection

Visual language

What most of the apps share became the prototype’s look. None of it comes from my own style.

  • Light ground, white cardsBevel, Fitbit, Future and Fi put content on rounded white cards over a pale grey.Ground #F2F2F4, cards with 20px corners.
  • Black while you trainPeloton, Ladder and Gymshark go dark during a session, so you can read it from the floor.Session screen in pure black.
  • One bright accentLime and green recur: Bevel’s rings, Ladder’s gauge, Peloton Strength+ and Runna.Lime #C6F24E, green #1F9D55 for text.
  • Big, heavy numbersEvery app sets its key number large and bold, often in a rounded face.Rounded system type, 700 weight.
  • Pill buttons, floating tabsVisible, Tempo and Runna use full pills. Apple Fitness and Gentler Streak float the tab bar.Pills everywhere, a floating tab bar.
  • Rest in blueRunna marks rest in blue, apart from the work.Rest #6AA8FF.

04

Guidelines

Where the apps differ, these pick a side.

  1. Say it in words firstMost apps lead with a score. Visible and Tempo add a word. Lead with the word: “Steady” before any number.
  2. Compare to your usualKeep the ring everyone knows, but measure it against your own normal, the way Gentler Streak compares to a typical Friday.
  3. Three reasons, in plain rowsWhy today looks like this, as three short sentences with a direction, like Visible. Not a wall of metrics, like WHOOP.
  4. Time, now, nextWhile you train: the clock, the move and what comes after, as Peloton and Ladder do. Nothing else competes.
  5. Rest is a state, not a waitRest gets its own colour and a kind word. It is part of the session, not a gap in it.
  6. End with a questionSkip the confetti of Hevy and Bevel. Ask how it felt, like Runna, and let the answer shape tomorrow.

    One ring, because every app has one, but with a mark for your usual and a word in the middle instead of a score. One suggestion with one pill, then three reasons in rows. Guidelines 01, 02 and 03.

    Session

    Black, with a segmented bar on top and a ring around the clock, like Peloton and Ladder. The move, then what comes next. Rest turns the ring and the move name blue. Guidelines 04 and 05.

    Done

    A check mark, not confetti. One honest number and one question, and your answer changes tomorrow’s suggestion. Guideline 06.

    06

    Kept and cut

    • KeptThe ring, the light cards, the black session screen, the segmented bar, the “next” row, pill buttons and the floating tab bar. They’re familiar for a reason.
    • ChangedThe score became a word. The goal became your usual. Rest got its own colour. Confetti became a question.
    • CutCalories, streak counters, share sheets, trainer video, three rings at once and long lists of metrics. Each one asks for attention the moment doesn’t need.
