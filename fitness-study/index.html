A calmer fitness app

A study in four steps, made with Claude Code: how fitness and health apps show readiness, guide a session and end it; a moodboard of the patterns; six guidelines; and a small prototype that follows them.

01

Research

Four searches on Mobbin, eight iOS screens each, read one by one. The question was simple: what does a fitness app say to you on an ordinary day?

  • The dayActivity summaries with rings, bars and steps. Apple Fitness, Garmin Connect, Fitbit, Bevel, Gentler Streak.
  • The bodyReadiness and recovery scores and how they are explained. Oura, WHOOP, Visible, Tempo, Ultrahuman.
  • The sessionScreens while you train, and the screen right after. Peloton, Ladder, Runna, Hevy, Strava.

03

Guidelines

  1. Say it in words first“A steady day” before any number. Most apps lead with a score; the best ones (Visible, Tempo) add a word. Lead with the word.
  2. Compare to your usualA dashed line for your own normal beats a ring for someone else’s goal. Gentler Streak’s “above typical Friday” is the model.
  3. Three reasons, in plain rowsWhy today looks like this, as three short sentences with a direction. Never a wall of metrics.
  4. Time, now, nextWhile you train, the screen shows the clock, what you are doing and what comes after. Nothing else competes.
  5. Rest is a state, not a waitRest gets its own calm colour and a kind word. It is part of the session, not a gap in it.
  6. End with a questionSkip the confetti. Ask how it felt, and let the answer shape tomorrow. That is the reward.
