Ask the map

Ask for a walk the way you'd ask a friend. Real marked trails come back on a contour map. What the map can measure is kept apart from what Claude is guessing, so you know which is which.

Trails and places: © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL), through the Overpass API and Nominatim. Elevation: Terrain Tiles by Mapzen on AWS Open Data. Walking times by Naismith's rule; sunset by NOAA's formula.

How it works

  • Claude reads the askYour words become things a map can check: where, how long, a loop or not, and what you want to pass. Anything a map can't tell, like mud or crowds, is named as such.
  • The map does the factsMarked trails come from OpenStreetMap. Length, climb, walking time, the lakes and viewpoints along the way, and how high the land rises toward the sunset are all measured in your browser.
  • Claude picks and guessesClaude sees only those measured facts. It picks up to three and says why, and anything it can't know from the map is written as a guess and marked with ~.
  • No invented trailsEvery route on the map is a real, marked trail someone mapped. If nothing fits, it says which part of the ask it couldn't meet, rather than drawing one.
