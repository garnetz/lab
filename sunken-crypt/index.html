The Sunken Crypt

A dark action RPG in the spirit of Diablo 1. You go down through a generated, torchlit crypt beneath the drowned town of Hollowmere, with a lantern that runs out, skeletons in the dark and loot worth the risk. It's my first game. I designed and directed it, and Claude Code wrote it, in the Godot engine.

Play the first prototype

The prototype runs in your browser, with a mouse or a touch screen. The full game is a Mac app.

The brief

Depth over breadth. One cursed town and one dungeon done well, rather than a big world done thinly. That's also a scope a first-time developer and Claude can actually finish.

It should feel the way Diablo 1 did: dread, scarcity and silence broken by sudden violence, not the bright loot fountain of later games. The design doc names three feelings to protect in every decision.

  • DreadYou never see far. Sound and glowing eyes warn you before the monster appears.
  • WantEvery unidentified item on the floor might be the one.
  • ReturnDying hurts, the town is a relief, and going back down is a choice you make.

How it was made

I set the vision, judged every build by playing it and said what felt wrong. Claude Code wrote the code, the scenes, the shaders, the tests and even the sound.

  1. A design doc firstVision, core loop, classes, items, story, art direction and a roadmap of milestones, each ending in something playable. It also compared engines. Godot won over a browser game and over Unity or Unreal, because it has real lighting, shadows and animation and is free and open.
  2. A one-file browser prototypeBefore any engine, a single web page tested the feel: click to walk and fight, a torch, potions, stairs down. It's the one you can play from this page.
  3. The game, one milestone at a timeClaude Code built the Godot project and pushed it to GitHub. I opened it, pressed Play and wrote down what felt wrong. Each round of notes became a batch of fixes, such as a lantern handed over in town, held attacks and save slots that remember levels.
  4. A Mac app on every changeEvery change to the game builds a downloadable Mac app automatically, so the latest version was always one download away.

Light is the art direction

Diablo 1's darkness, lit the way Valheim does it: simple low-poly models with chunky, pixel-crisp textures, made beautiful by the lighting.

You carry the main light. Torches, braziers and spells are the only other sources, and beyond them is a faint, dark mist rather than flat black. The palette is desaturated browns, greys and bone, with two saturated accents, fire orange and blood red. Magic is the only cold blue. Later, light became a rule as well as a look. Your lantern burns oil, and when it goes out, the dark gets dangerous.

The game today

Two classes, a warrior and a rogue, four kinds of skeletons, champions and a named guardian on every level, and magic, rare and unique items to hunt. Between trips there's a town with traders, quests and a journal.

Built to grow

  • Rules apart from visualsThe game's rules know nothing about graphics or sound. A click becomes an order, the rules decide what happens and the visuals draw it. That keeps co-op possible later without a rewrite.
  • Tested on every changeAutomated tests check rules like damage, loot rolls and saves. All the numbers for monsters and items sit in data files, so they can be balanced without touching the code.
  • Its own soundAlongside free sound libraries, Claude Code wrote scripts that made the game's own effects and music: dark ambient tracks for the crypt and a guitar theme for the title.
